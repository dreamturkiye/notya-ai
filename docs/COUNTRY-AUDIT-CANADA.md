# Country audit: Canada (`ca`)

Audit of the Canada pack (`countries/ca/`, language form `en-CA`, served at `/ca`) against Canada's own standards. Written 2026-10-09 on branch `audit/ca`, from the development branch `feat/ulke-butun` at `d7bc33a0`.

**What this is and is not.** A machine read official pages and compared them with the pack and the kit. Nobody in Canada (no clinician, no lawyer, no native editor) has read the pack or this document. Every standard below carries the page it was read on; where no official page could be opened, the item is marked **UNVERIFIED** and says what was found instead. Nothing here is a statement that the product complies with any law or standard. Legal matters are written as questions for a lawyer.

**What was changed.** Only files in `countries/ca/`, that pack's test, the pack's generated record (`docs/COUNTRY-PACK-CANADA.md`, regenerated by the repository's own command) and this document with its images. Nothing in the kit, the shared English set, another country's folder, `main` or any Turkish file was touched. Nothing was merged or deployed, and no remote database or setting was touched.

## Summary

- **Five fixes made in the country's folder** (section "Fixes made"): two role names, the identifier label, a missing time zone, the example phone number.
- **Most important findings that cannot be fixed in the country's folder** (section "Core"):
  1. **British Columbia stopped changing its clocks in 2026.** The time-zone data of the Node runtime on this machine (2025b) still moves `America/Vancouver` back an hour on 1 November 2026. Until the runtime of the deployment (and each user's browser) carries the 2026b data or later, appointment times of an account in British Columbia will be one hour wrong from that day.
  2. **A typed comma is read as a decimal point** in every number field of the tools and of the intake form. In English Canada the comma separates thousands, so "1,250" typed into a field is read as 1.25.
  3. **Date fields are the browser's, not the pack's.** Date of birth and every other date picker show the order of the browser's own language: seen as `03/04/2019` in a browser set to United States English, beside screens that write year-month-day.
  3a. **The patient's day buttons show the year and no day** ("Sat 2026-") because the kit's short date is the first five characters of the written date, and Canada's date is year-first: a patient asking for an appointment cannot tell one Saturday from the next.
  4. **The identifier field asks a phone for the numeric keyboard**, and health numbers in at least two provinces contain letters.
  5. **Time zones are shown by their database names** ("America/St Johns", "America/Regina"), not by the names people use.
- **For a lawyer before any real patient:** which health-privacy law applies in each province and what a vendor must sign; whether a health card number may be asked for at all; French in Quebec; the guardian age (Quebec 14, New Brunswick 16, elsewhere no fixed age); recording consent; medical-device status.

Counts for Part B are at the end of that part. The screens were seen on a local build (Part C); the walk-through passed 329 of 329.

## Part A — the standards sheet

Each line: the standard, then the source. "Opened" means the page was read during this audit on 2026-10-09.

### A1. Dates

- **All-numeric dates are written year-month-day with hyphens: `2026-10-09`.** The Government of Canada's Data Reference Standard on Date and Time Format requires "(calendar) year-month-day" with a four-digit year, two-digit month and two-digit day, cites ISO 8601-1:2019, took effect on 2024-09-27 and binds federal departments' data and systems. Opened: https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/enabling-interoperability/gc-enterprise-data-reference-standards/data-reference-standard-date-time-format.html
- The federal Translation Bureau gives the same rule for everyday writing in both official languages: numeric dates are year-month-day, joined by hyphens ("2008-03-04 represents March 4, 2008"); in words, English writes month-day-year ("January 2, 2008") and French day-month-year. Opened: https://www.noslangues-ourlanguages.gc.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/date-order-of-elements
- The older federal standard TBITS 36, "All-Numeric Representation of Dates and Times", exists (https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=17284) but the page refused the request. **UNVERIFIED**: its text and its current status.
- **Health sector.** No single national rule for dates in a clinical record was found. What was found: a regional health authority's policy (Southern Health-Santé Sud, Manitoba, policy ORG.1410.PL.105) requires a four-digit year, a **three-letter month** and a two-digit day ("2015-Aug-05", "05-Aug-2015") "to avoid confusion", citing ISO 8601; opened: https://staffnet.southernhealth.ca/wp-content/uploads/Standard-Date-and-Time.pdf . The nursing regulator of British Columbia requires the date and time on every entry and fixes no format; opened: https://www.bccnm.ca/Documents/standards_practice/nurses/Nurses_Documentation.pdf
- **Ambiguity risk.** Canada lives with three orders at once: the federal year-first form, the month-first form of English writing and of the United States, and the day-first form of French writing and of Britain. A date such as 03/04/2026 can therefore be read two ways by two Canadians; the year-first form with hyphens is the only all-numeric form that cannot. That reasoning is this audit's; the only official page found that states the purpose in words is the health authority's policy above. **Whether a clinic wants the month as letters (as that policy does) is for a local clinical lead.**

### A2. Clock

- **Everyday English: 12-hour with "a.m." and "p.m."** The 24-hour system "is used in specialized fields (such as the military) and in French Canada", is suggested for bilingual and international documents, and is recommended to avoid confusing noon and midnight. Translation Bureau, opened: https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/time-of-day-elapsed-time
- Federal data: 24-hour, `00` to `23` (the Data Reference Standard above).
- **Clinical documentation.** No national rule was found. The Manitoba health authority's policy above requires the 24-hour clock in records ("2145 hours or 21:45 hours"). **Whether clinic screens and notes should be 24-hour is for a local clinical lead.**

### A3. First day of the week

- **UNVERIFIED from an official source.** No federal or provincial page stating the first day of the week on calendars was found. The platform's locale data for `en-CA` (Unicode CLDR, as shipped in Node 22: `firstDay: 7`) says Sunday; that is an industry data set, not an authority.

### A4. Numbers

- **The decimal marker in English Canada is the period**, with a zero before it for values under 1 ("0.64"). Translation Bureau, opened: https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/numbers-decimal-fractions
- **Thousands.** The same page: the Canadian Standards Association "advises against the use of commas to separate groups of three digits"; a space is used ("50 005"). **Money is the stated exception** and keeps commas ("$502,459").

### A5. Currency

- Canadian dollar, ISO code CAD, two decimals; the dollar sign stands before the figure ("$502,459"). Translation Bureau page above. How to mark the currency where another dollar could be meant ("C$", "CAD") was not read on an official page: **UNVERIFIED**.

### A6. Time zones

- **Six zones**: Newfoundland, Atlantic, Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific. Daylight saving time runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November and "is determined by provincial legislation"; "exceptions may exist in certain municipalities". National Research Council, opened: https://nrc.canada.ca/en/certifications-evaluations-standards/canadas-official-time/time-zones-daylight-saving-time (the page's footnotes for Saskatchewan, Nunavut and Yukon were not returned, so the detail for **Saskatchewan is UNVERIFIED** from this page; the time-zone database gives it one time all year, `America/Regina`).
- **Yukon keeps one time all year**: "Starting on November 1 [2020], Yukon Standard Time will be permanently seven hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC -7)." Government of Yukon, opened: https://open.yukon.ca/information/a85a97cc-99fd-45a5-ae7f-81ca844c7805/resource/f7b7f5d7-2f35-4563-9d52-13a57b0e67a6/download/20-269.html
- **British Columbia stopped changing its clocks in 2026.** "B.C. has adopted permanent year-round daylight saving time, or Pacific time"; 8 March 2026 "was the final time change in British Columbia"; the time is seven hours behind UTC. Parts of the province differ (the Peace region already kept that time; the East Kootenay follows Alberta; Golden changes once more on 1 November 2026). Government of British Columbia, opened: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/celebrating-british-columbia/daylight-saving-time
- The time-zone database carries the British Columbia change from **release 2026b (2026-04-22)**. IANA, opened: https://www.iana.org/time-zones/releases/2026b
- **How appointment times should be shown.** No official rule was found. Sense: an appointment is a wall-clock time at the clinic; a doctor sees it in the clinic's zone; a patient in another zone needs the zone named. For a local lead.

### A7. Units for the body

- **The International System of Units is the legal basis** of every unit of measurement in Canada (Weights and Measures Act, s. 4(1)); Canadian units (Schedule II) may still be used in trade. Opened: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/W-6/page-1.html
- **Weight in kilograms for anything a dose depends on**; patients often state pounds, and the confusion causes harm. ISMP Canada (medication-safety body, supported by Health Canada) describes a child's weight of "18" taken as pounds and recommends kilograms with the unit said aloud. Opened: https://safemedicationuse.ca/newsletter/newsletter_WeightKg.html
- Height in centimetres and temperature in degrees Celsius follow from SI; that patients commonly give height in feet and inches is general knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** from an official page.

### A8. Laboratory units

- **Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio: mg/mmol**, with categories under 3.0, 3.0 to 30.0 and over 30.0 mg/mmol; **creatinine: µmol/L**; **haemoglobin: g/L**; eGFR in mL/min per 1.73 m²; blood pressure in mmHg. Government of British Columbia, BC Guidelines, Chronic Kidney Disease, opened: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/practitioner-professional-resources/bc-guidelines/chronic-kidney-disease
- **Cholesterol (LDL-C, non-HDL-C): mmol/L**; A1C as a percentage; urine ACR in mg/mmol. BC Guidelines, Diabetes Care, opened: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/practitioner-professional-resources/bc-guidelines/diabetes
- **Glucose in mmol/L: UNVERIFIED from the page** (the guideline's glucose table is an image that could not be read); it is the unit general knowledge gives.
- Other values a tool of this pack takes: **prostate-specific antigen in µg/L, C-reactive protein in mg/L, erythrocyte sedimentation rate in mm/h — UNVERIFIED** (no official page opened). These pages are one province's; laboratories are provincial, and **a local clinical lead confirms the units for the provinces the product is offered in.**

### A9. Blood pressure

### A10. Emergency and health-advice numbers

- **9-1-1.** "Either Basic or Enhanced 9-1-1 service is available to almost all subscribers in Canada." **Nunavut has no 9-1-1 service**: emergency services are reached through a regular telephone number there. CRTC, opened: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/911/can.htm
- **8-1-1** is the code the CRTC assigned "for access to non-urgent health care telephone triage services" (Telecom Decision CRTC 2005-39, opened: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2005/dt2005-39.htm). It is a provincial service and not every jurisdiction uses the code: a 2023 overview by the pan-Canadian health technology agency lists 811-named lines in Alberta, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, the Northwest Territories, Ontario, Prince Edward Island and Quebec, and other names in British Columbia (HealthLink BC, which also serves Yukon), Manitoba (Health Links), Saskatchewan (HealthLine) and Nunavut. Opened: https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/attachments/2023-10/canadian_telephone_triage_programs_infographic.pdf . **Which number each of those four dials is UNVERIFIED.**
- **9-8-8**, the Suicide Crisis Helpline, by call or text, at all hours; and "if you're in immediate danger or need urgent medical support, call 9-1-1". Government of Canada, opened: https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/mental-health-services/mental-health-get-help.html

### A22. Age of consent to treatment

### Questions for a lawyer

   - Provincial laws apply instead of it or beside it. Opened: the federal order that exempts Ontario's health information custodians from Part 1 of PIPEDA for what happens "within the Province of Ontario" (SOR/2005-399, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2005-399/page-1.html). ## Part B — what the product does today

How the kit works, read from the code (all read-only for this audit):

- **Dates shown**: the pack's pattern. `lib/ulke/arayuz/bicim.ts` (`tarihYaz`) and `lib/ulke/uygulama/zaman.ts` (`gunYazDesenle`) write digits into `bicim.tarihDeseni`; nothing is taken from the browser.
- **Dates typed**: two kinds. The appointment form takes typed text in the pack's pattern (`gunCoz`, `components/ulke/uygulama/Takvim.tsx:237`). Everything else is the browser's own date picker, `<input type="date">`: date of birth (`Hastalar.tsx:132`, `OnBuro.tsx:214`), a tool's date fields (`Araclar.tsx:130`), the follow-up day (`AracKayitlari.tsx:90`), the end of cover (`KlinikYetkiler.tsx:93`), a date question of the intake form (`portal/HastaFormu.tsx:146`). A browser's date picker shows the order of the **browser's** language, not the pack's.
- **Times shown**: the pack's `uygulama.saatBicimi`; for 12 the words come from the platform's data for the pack's locale (`Intl.DateTimeFormat('en-CA')`, which writes "2:30 p.m."). **Times typed**: the browser's `<input type="time">`, whose 12 or 24-hour face is again the browser's.
- **Numbers and money**: the pack's two separators (`lib/ulke/arayuz/sayi.ts`); the currency sign and its side are text of the catalogue. **Numbers typed**: `lib/ulke/araclar/girdi.ts:35` and `components/ulke/portal/HastaFormu.tsx:64` replace a comma with a point before reading the number.
- **Units**: the pack's (`uygulama.birimler`, `labBirimleri`); the kit converts with fixed factors and never shows a measured field without its unit.
- **Time zones**: the account's own, one of the pack's list (`lib/ulke/uygulama/saatDilimi.ts`); the list is shown by database names (`components/ulke/uygulama/Ayarlar.tsx:85`); every conversion uses the platform's time-zone data.
- **Phone**: the pack's rule (`telefon.cepGecerliMi`) and example; no formatting is applied to what is typed.
- **Names**: one field, "Full name"; sorting by the pack's locale (`Intl.Collator`).

## Core — findings in the kit, not fixed

Each names the file and what would have to change. None was changed.

1. **British Columbia's time from 1 November 2026 (deployment and kit).** Every conversion (`lib/ulke/uygulama/zaman.ts`, `lib/ulke/arayuz/bicim.ts`, `lib/ulke/uygulama/gun.ts`) reads the platform's time-zone data. Node 22.22.0 on this machine reports `process.versions.tz = 2025b`, and for `America/Vancouver` on 2026-12-01 it gives UTC−08:00; the province is at UTC−07:00 all year (A6; IANA 2026b). To change: the Node runtime of the Canada deployment must carry tz data 2026b or later (a newer Node release, or Node started with an updated ICU data file), and a test of the kit should fail the build when `America/Vancouver` in December 2026 is not UTC−07:00. The browser converts too (`bicim.ts` runs on the client), so an out-of-date browser shows the wrong hour even with a correct server: answering times from the server already written, or warning on a mismatch, would close that. Until then no account in British Columbia should rely on appointment times after that day.
2. **A typed comma is read as a decimal point (clinical safety).** `lib/ulke/araclar/girdi.ts:35` and `:48` (`v.trim().replace(',', '.')`) and `components/ulke/portal/HastaFormu.tsx:64` (`sayiCoz`). In a pack whose thousands separator is the comma (`bicim.binlikAyraci === ','`: Canada and every English-speaking pack), "1,250" becomes 1.25 without a word. A field's range check catches some cases and not others. To change: read the two separators from the pack; where the comma is the pack's thousands separator, refuse a number that contains one (or read it as thousands) and say so on the screen.
3. **Date pickers follow the browser, not the pack.** `components/ulke/uygulama/Hastalar.tsx:132` (date of birth), `OnBuro.tsx:160, 214`, `Araclar.tsx:130`, `AracKayitlari.tsx:90`, `KlinikYetkiler.tsx:93`, `components/ulke/portal/HastaFormu.tsx:146`. To change: the typed field the appointment form already has (`Takvim.tsx:237`, placeholder from the pack, read by `gunCoz`), or a picker drawn by the kit in the pack's pattern.
4. **Time fields follow the browser** (`Takvim.tsx:241, 623, 627, 640, 641`, `OnBuro.tsx:161`). To change: a field in the pack's clock.
5. **The identifier field asks for a numeric keyboard and digits.** `components/ulke/uygulama/Hastalar.tsx:145`: `inputMode="numeric"`. To change: take the keyboard hint from the pack (for example `ulusalKimlik.hane === 0` or a new flag means plain text).
6. **Time zones are listed by database name.** `components/ulke/uygulama/Ayarlar.tsx:85` writes `d.replace(/_/g, ' ')`: "America/St Johns", "America/Regina", "America/Whitehorse". To change: an optional name per zone in the pack's catalogue (for example "Newfoundland", "Saskatchewan (no clock change)", "Yukon"), falling back to the database name.
7. **One emergency number, no health-advice line.** `uygulama.portal.acilNumara` is one string. Canada has 9-1-1 almost everywhere, none in Nunavut, and a non-urgent health line whose number differs by province. To change: an optional second, non-urgent line in the pack's portal settings (a slot, empty until a local source confirms it per province), and a way to state "no 9-1-1 here".
8. **One thousands separator for numbers and for money** (`lib/ulke/tipler.ts`, `BicimKurallari`). The federal writing rule separates the two (A4). To change, if a local editor wants it: a separate separator for money.
9. **The record's notes for Canada live in a shared script.** `scripts/ulke-en-kayit.mts`, the `ca` entry (`kimlikNotu`, `saatNotu`, `telefonNotu`, `ekNotlar`): after this audit the regenerated record still carries the old sentence `Label "Provincial health card number"` beside the new label, and calls the zone list and the date choice "unverified" without the sources found here. To change: those four strings.
10. **The walk-through and the record cannot see any of the above**: they read what the pack says, not what a browser of another language draws. The walk-through passed 329 of 329 with findings 2, 3, 11 and 12 on the screens it walked.
11. **The day buttons of the patient's "Ask for an appointment" show the year and no day** (seen: "Sat 2026-", `docs/audit/ca/10-patient-portal.jpg`). `components/ulke/portal/PortalSayfasi.tsx:51`, `portalGunu`: the short form of a day is `gunYaz(gun).slice(0, 5)`, the first five characters of the written date. That is the day and the month where a pattern begins with them (DD.MM.YYYY, DD/MM/YYYY, MM/DD/YYYY) and **the year and a hyphen where the pattern is year-first**, which is Canada's and only Canada's. A patient who asks for an appointment sees the same text on every button of a weekday and cannot tell one Saturday from the next. To change: build the short form from the pattern with its year part removed (for Canada "10-10"), not from a fixed number of characters; and a test that the short form of two different days of one year differs in every pack.
12. **On a phone, the patient's sent intake form draws each answer in a column one letter wide** (seen: `docs/audit/ca/11-intake-form.jpg`). `components/ulke/portal/HastaFormu.tsx`, the read-only view. Not a matter of Canada: any country, any answer. To change: put the answer under its question at narrow widths.

It holds products authorised for sale in Canada (human, veterinary, disinfectant, radiopharmaceutical) with status marketed, approved, cancelled or dormant, each with its Drug Identification Number, brand name, company, active ingredients and strengths, form, route, schedule and therapeutic class. "The DPD is updated nightly." Opened: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/drug-product-database.html

  - **API**: `https://health-products.canada.ca/api/drug/` with JSON or XML, in English or French; endpoints for product, active ingredient, company, form, packaging, route, schedule, status, therapeutic class. No rate limit or licence is stated on the documentation page. Opened: https://health-products.canada.ca/api/documentation/dpd-documentation-en.html
  - **Licence**: the dataset "Drug Product Database - All Files" is published by Health Canada on the Open Government Portal under the **"Open Government Licence - Canada"** (opened: https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/bf55e42a-63cb-4556-bfd8-44f26e5a36fe). The licence allows to "copy, modify, publish, translate, adapt, distribute" for any lawful purpose including commercial ones, asks for the attribution "Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada", gives no warranty, forbids suggesting official status or endorsement, and **does not cover "third party rights the Information Provider is not authorized to license"** nor names, crests and logos (opened: https://open.canada.ca/en/open-government-licence-canada). The database's own terms page (https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/drug-product-database/terms-conditions.html) could not be opened: **UNVERIFIED** whether it adds anything.
- **Canadian Clinical Drug Data Set (CCDD)**, Health Canada: "a drug terminology and coding system" built from the DPD for electronic prescribing and records. CSV files, released **monthly**: therapeutic moiety (the substance), non-proprietary therapeutic product (substance, strength, form), manufactured product (the branded product), their relationships, in English and French, with data dictionaries and editorial guidelines. Licence: **"Open Government Licence - Canada"**. Opened: https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/3e0a7b9e-a5e9-4131-bde4-ac685a1f1a38

**Dosing information.** The DPD links each human product to its **product monograph**, the document that holds the authorised dosing. The pages opened make no statement on who owns a monograph's text; a monograph is written by the product's manufacturer, and the licence above excludes third-party rights, so **whether monograph text may be reused in a commercial product is a question for a lawyer, and this audit assumes it may not without permission.** Structured dosing data comes from licensed compendia. The main Canadian one is the Compendium of Pharmaceuticals and Specialties (CPS) of the Canadian Pharmacists Association (its page, https://www.pharmacists.ca/products-services/cps-drug-information/, could not be opened: **UNVERIFIED**); commercial drug databases with Canadian content exist beside it (from general knowledge, **UNVERIFIED**: Lexicomp, Micromedex, First Databank, Vigilance Santé). **Any of them needs a licence**, and none was contacted.

**What the pack would need to take a names list.** The slots exist already, empty and switched off, in the shared English set (`countries/_dil/en/araclar/yuvalar.ts`), and each says it waits on the register: `prescription`, `medicine-interactions`, `polypharmacy`, `anticoagulation-review`, `antiseizure-monitoring`, `psychotropic-monitoring`. A names list alone fills none of them: each also needs content a register does not hold (a prescription form and its rules, interaction data, criteria, monitoring schedules). What a names list could serve by itself is recognition: spelling a medicine's name correctly in a note that the doctor dictated. That would be a new, separate slot of this pack (a generated data file under `countries/ca/`, with its source, licence line, release date and a refresh script), not a change to the six above, and the kit has no place that reads such a list today (Core).

**Risks.** A names list is not clinical guidance but it is clinical-safety material: look-alike and sound-alike names, a product withdrawn after the list was taken (the register changes nightly, a copy ages), brand names that mean different substances in different countries, French names beside English ones. A list must never be used to "correct" what a doctor said into another medicine; at most it flags a name it does not know. The attribution line is required, and no wording may suggest Health Canada endorses the product. Dose arithmetic stays the doctor's (the note instruction already says "Do no dose arithmetic").

**Recommended route, for the owner to decide.** Take **names only** from the CCDD's therapeutic-moiety and manufactured-product files (monthly, Open Government Licence – Canada), cross-checked against the DPD's status so that only marketed products are in it, into a new generated file of the Canada pack that carries the attribution line and its release date and is refreshed by a script on a schedule. Use it only to recognise and spell names in a draft note, never to suggest or check a dose, and only after a Canadian pharmacist or clinician has reviewed how it is used. Leave dosing, interactions and every monitoring schedule as the empty slots they are until the owner licenses a compendium.
