# Doctor tools and specialties: United States (`us`)

Audit of every doctor tool and every specialty the United States build offers, against what doctors in the United States use (NOTYA araclar-denetim, 2026-10-10). Branch `araclar-denetim/us`, from `feat/ulke-butun` at `e254ebf8`. The same decisions as data: `docs/araclar-denetim/us-kararlar.json`.

**The answer in one paragraph.** The United States build shows 45 tools today, keeps 53 more as empty placeholders that no screen shows ("slots"), and leaves 2 tiles of the kit out. Almost all of it is the Turkish product's specialty checklists in translation. Checked one by one on 2026-10-10: **5 can stay as they are, 32 need a change** (12 live tools, 18 slots, the 2 absent tiles), **1 must come off** (the ESI triage record: the scale belongs to the Emergency Nurses Association and may not be used without written permission), and **62 could not be checked against a US source today** and wait for a US clinician. Seven live tools show something wrong or misleading for a doctor here (kidney referral flags, a kidney "risk" colour without the urine test, the hearing-loss grade, the expected-height range, the last day of an antibiotic course, the ASA classes, the return-to-sport steps), and two more carry a trap or a licence problem (the CRP unit in DAS28; BI-RADS). The larger gap is what is missing: **17 of the 40 roles see no tool except the patient's page** — family medicine, cardiology, obstetrics and gynecology, psychiatry, neurology, gastroenterology, physical medicine and rehabilitation, and all ten clinic roles — and none of the tools that the US bodies read today build their recommendations on exists (BMI, blood-pressure category, eGFR, cardiovascular risk, PHQ-9, GAD-7, growth charts, the CDC schedules). I propose **a US core set of 16 tools** for every doctor, each tied to a US body, **15 specialty tools** with a US source read today, and 30 further candidates for a clinician to confirm. On specialties: of the 30 doctor specialties **25 keep their name, 5 should be renamed, 0 are removed, 10 should be added**; of the 10 clinic roles **5 are kept, 1 is removed** (a duplicate), **4 are kinds of practice that are no recognised specialty** (a lawyer), and **8 professions should be added** — first of all nurse practitioners and physician assistants.

## How to read this

- **What was done.** The pack was evaluated as the build evaluates it, to list exactly what a US account can see. Every tool's arithmetic and limits were read in the kit's code (`lib/ulke/araclar/tanimlar/`) and run on worked examples. US sources were then opened on 2026-10-10 and compared. Nothing in the product was changed: this document and the data file are the whole change.
- **The verdicts.** *keep*: checked against a source read today and nothing needs to change (or it is a screen of the product with no clinical content). *revise*: something must change, and the row says what. *remove*: not usable here. ***unverified***: no US source could be opened today that confirms or contradicts the tool; the row gives the direction I lean in, and **that leaning is not a decision** — a US clinician makes it.
- **Why so many are "unverified".** Two reasons, both plain. First, 21 of the live tools are the product's own checklists and date cards: they hold no formula and no national content, so no web page can say whether a US surgeon or oncologist wants them. Second, the search allowance this session shared with other jobs ran out after 17 searches, and from then on only addresses already seen, and government sites, could be opened: most professional societies (rheumatology, urology, dermatology, otolaryngology, obstetrics, cardiology journals) and PubMed could not be read. The exact list is at the end.
- **No clinical content was written from memory.** Where a number appears below it was read on the page cited, or it is what the product's own code does.
- **Nobody of the United States has read this.** No clinician, no lawyer. Nothing here says the product meets any law, standard or guideline, and no body named here has reviewed or endorsed anything.

## The findings that matter most

1. **Kidney tool (internal medicine): referral flags attributed to KDIGO that KDIGO 2024 does not state that way.** The screen says "KDIGO criterion for referral to a nephrologist" for any albumin ratio above 300 mg/g, for a GFR fall of more than 25% in a year, and for "the very-high-risk cell". The KDIGO 2024 list read today has a sustained fall of more than 20%, a ratio of 300 mg/g or more *together with hematuria* (or consistently above 700 mg/g), eGFR below 30, and a 5-year kidney-failure risk of 3 to 5%; the "cell" is not on it. A doctor is told a guideline says something it does not.
2. **Both kidney tools show a risk colour when the urine test is missing.** eGFR 75 with nothing else typed reads "Low risk (green cell)". The cell is defined by two results; with one missing the tool silently assumes the better category.
3. **DAS28 (rheumatology): the CRP field takes mg/L and tells the doctor to multiply by 10 by hand.** A US result typed as it came from a laboratory that reports mg/dL gives a lower score (worked example: 3.43 instead of 4.04). The formula itself could not be checked today.
4. **Hearing tool: "within normal limits" up to 25 dB.** The US reference table (ASHA, from the same 1981 paper the tool cites) calls 16 to 25 dB a *slight hearing loss*.
5. **Antibiotic course: the "last day" is one day late** when the first day counts as day 1 (7 days from October 1 shows October 8). This is the kit's arithmetic, so it is true in every country build.
6. **Two live tools use other people's property without a licence.** ESI (Emergency Nurses Association: written permission required) and BI-RADS (American College of Radiology: licence agreement required for commercial software). For mammography the BI-RADS tool also lacks the category wording and the breast-density statement that federal rules require in every report.
7. **Expected height: the range is a British one.** The tool shows 8.5 cm either side; the US family physicians' journal says 10 cm (4 in). **ASA class:** no ASA VI, and "E" offered as if it were a class. **Return to sport:** not the CDC's six steps.
8. **Good news on PHQ-9 and GAD-7.** The slot is closed "for the licence question". The forms themselves say "No permission required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute". The USPSTF names both instruments. They belong in the core set.

## What the kit lacks to apply these decisions for the United States alone

Read in `countries/_dil/en/araclar/index.ts`, `countries/_dil/en/girdi.ts`, `lib/ulke/araclar/tipler.ts` and `countries/us/ayarlar.ts`. A country that takes the shared English set can today do exactly three things to a tool: switch it off (`kapali`), change words on it (`degisen`), and rename a unit. Everything else below is missing. And before any of it: **none of the core set exists in the kit's catalogue** — each new tool needs its arithmetic written once in the kit (country-neutral, with its published source) before any country can switch it on.

1. **A country cannot say who sees a tool.** The roles of every tool are written once in the shared English set for all five countries. The US pack cannot show the kidney tool to family medicine, the hearing tool to audiologists, or the follow-up list to everybody.
2. **A country cannot have a tool of its own.** The pack's tool list is assembled by the shared set, and what a country hands the set (`EnAraclarGirdisi`) has no field for a tool of its own. A US-only tool (PREVENT, the Medicare wellness-visit list, the mammography statements) would have to be added to the shared set and then switched off by hand in the other four countries.
3. **A country cannot change a tool's numbers or band names** unless the kit left them open as parameters, and only eight slot tools do. The hearing-loss grades, the expected-height range and the return-to-sport steps are fixed in the kit. Needed: country parameters or country variants for those three.
4. **A country cannot add, split or merge a role, only rename one.** The 40 keys are shared by five countries and a test holds every pack to exactly those 40. Ten specialties and eight professions to add, one role to split (heart and vascular surgery) and one to fold away (clinic dermatology) all wait on this.
5. **A role is one thing; here it is two.** A nurse practitioner or physician assistant has a profession *and* works in a specialty. The account has one role, and every doctor role's instruction to the model begins "You are an experienced attending physician".
6. **Tools are gated by role only, never by the patient's age.** The adult core tools would show for pediatrics and children's tools for adult medicine. The branch rule of this product (one specialty's content must not show for another) applies to age the same way.
7. **Units inside a tool.** No height in feet and inches; no weight field that stays in kilograms when the country's unit is pounds; no unit choice on a single field (CRP in mg/L or mg/dL). The first two were recorded by the earlier audit; the third is new.
8. **No place for data with a date.** Growth-chart tables, the immunization schedules, the diagnosis codes and the USPSTF list are not text and not arithmetic: they are published data sets with a release date and a credit line. The kit has no shape for "a dated data set from a named source", the same gap the earlier audit found for medicine names.
9. **No place for a rights holder's notice or a licence state.** A tool has one citation line. PHQ-9's credit line, the ACR's trademark notice, or "licensed from … until …" have nowhere to live, and a slot cannot record that its licence was asked for or granted.
10. **No state.** Consent ages, recording consent and return-to-play rules differ by state; the pack has one value for the country.
