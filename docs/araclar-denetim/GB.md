# United Kingdom (`gb`): which doctor tools and specialties to keep, change, remove or add

## Second pass (2026-10-10)

The first pass ran out of its search allowance early and several NICE pages failed to load: 43 of the 100 verdicts were left "unverified" (20 of them on tools that are switched on), and the formulas of the shared kit were not checked against any publication. This pass had an allowance of its own (120 searches; 85 were used) and went back to the sources. It changed this document and the data file and nothing else: no code, pack, test or kit file. Rows it resolved carry `pass: 2` in `gb-kararlar.json`; in the tables of Part 1 a verdict cell that says "second pass" was changed here. Everything under the heading "First pass" is the first pass as it was written, except those verdict cells.

**How the sources were read.** Through a reading tool that returns the text of a page. Every figure taken from a source is as that tool returned it; nothing was written from memory. Before any number in the code is changed, a clinician in the United Kingdom opens the source again. Nobody of the United Kingdom (no clinician, no lawyer) has read this, and no body named here has reviewed or endorsed anything. A source in square brackets with "P2" in front, like [P2-NG12], is in the table "Sources read in the second pass" at the end.

**What was not checked again.** The arithmetic of the shared kit was checked against primary publications by the second passes of the United States, Australia and New Zealand, and those results are carried here by reference, not repeated: the PASI, EASI, SCORAD and DAS28 formulas; the 28 joints; the EASI weights for a child under 8 and the EASI bands; the antibiotic "last day"; the ASA class list; the kidney tool's "low risk" with no urine result; the training-ratio warning; the dose tool's trailing zeros; and which lists hold no number. They are marked "US second pass", "AU second pass" or "NZ second pass": `docs/araclar-denetim/US.md` on `araclar-denetim/us` (commit `5a19decf`), `AU.md` on `araclar-denetim/au` (`db93ed45`), `NZ.md` on `araclar-denetim/nz` (`b77460ce`), each under "Second pass (2026-10-10)" [P2-US], [P2-AU], [P2-NZ]. The searches of this pass went to what is specific to the United Kingdom.

Two omissions found in this pass, not wrong results: the fracture and cast follow-up has no item for the risk of venous thromboembolism, and NICE NG89 asks clinicians to consider prevention for people with a lower limb immobilised [P2-NG89]; the orthopaedic post-operative list has none either, and NICE offers prevention after planned hip and knee replacement [P2-NG89].

### Checked against a source and found right

- **DAS28 bands.** NICE's own text gives: above 5.1 high disease activity, 3.2 to 5.1 moderate, below 3.2 low, below 2.6 remission [P2-TA195]. The code cuts at exactly those four points (below 2.6, below 3.2, up to 5.1, above). Both formulas: US second pass. NICE NG100, read again, names DAS28 as the example score and prints no figure and no unit for C-reactive protein [NG100]: that British laboratories report it in mg/L is still not confirmed by a source read.
- **The 28 joints**, the **PASI formula**, **EASI for a patient of 8 or over**, the **SCORAD formula** (all US second pass).
- **The British hearing descriptors, the NICE asymmetry rule, the concussion stages and day limits, the ±7 cm of the growth chart, the NICE kidney categories and referral list**: the first pass's reading is confirmed, figure for figure, on a second reading.
- **The pack's laboratory units**, which the pack itself marks unverified: mg/mmol for the urine albumin ratio and g/L for haemoglobin [P2-NG203]; HbA1c in mmol/mol [NG28]. Not confirmed in this pass: µmol/L for creatinine, mmol/L for glucose and cholesterol.
- **PSA rate of change with no threshold** fits what NICE prints for active surveillance: kinetics are monitored and no limit is stated [P2-NG131ALG].
- **Removing the American triage scale** stands. The British system is licensed: software developers and end users take a licence through a portal run for the Advanced Life Support Group and the Manchester Triage Group [P2-MTSLIC].
- **Inhaler technique as a tool** has national backing: the joint asthma guideline asks for the person's technique to be observed at every asthma review [P2-NG245]. COPD: the guideline asks for a self-management plan and an exacerbation action plan [P2-NG115].
- **The psychiatry rule of the first pass** (no risk score, no risk level) is repeated by a second national source: the NHS Talking Therapies manual says clinicians should not sort people into low, medium or high risk, and that standard risk questions are "very poor predictors of risk when used in isolation" [P2-TTMANUAL].
- **Dose volume rounded to 0.1 mL**: no British source read states a rounding step, for or against.

### Every verdict that changed, and why

**(a) 14 live tools: unverified → keep, as record lists.** Each is the same shared tool the United States, Australian and New Zealand second passes opened in the code: tick-boxes, choices and dates, with no formula, weight, cut-off, band, grade, interval or stage, so no guideline can confirm or contradict them. All are switched on in the United Kingdom (the pack closes four tools only, and none of these). Their wording still waits for a British clinician. In the data file: `recordOnly: true`.

Airway note (`hava-yolu-notu`) · Post-operative pain follow-up (`postop-agri`) · Checklist after a neurosurgical operation (`noro-postop`) · Seizure and consciousness follow-up (`nobet-bilinc`) · Wound, drain and stitches follow-up (`yara-dren-izlem`) · Insulin and thyroid treatment: date card (`rejim-karti`) · Chest drain and wound follow-up (`toraks-tup-yara`) · Vascular graft and wound follow-up (`greft-yara-izlem`) · Otoscopy note (`otoskopi-notu`) · Dialysis session and next date (`diyaliz-seans`) · Treatment cycle counter (`kur-sayaci`) · Side effects checklist (`toksisite-listesi`) · Wound, graft and flap follow-up (`plastik-yara-greft`) · Examination queue (`tetkik-kuyrugu`)

One note for the clinician who reads them: the seizure and consciousness list has the item "Glasgow Coma Scale score recorded"; NICE's head injury guideline asks for the three responses (eyes, verbal, motor) to be recorded separately with the total [P2-NG232].

The critical conditions checklist (`kritik-yol`) was already "keep" and is now marked a record list too. Five more record lists keep the first pass's "revise", because a British source asks for an item or a wording they lack: the three pre-operative lists, the paediatric surgery list and the antithrombotic date card.

## Part 2: tools to add, and the United Kingdom's core set

**What "core" means here.** In Türkiye the tools every doctor shares are workflow tools (prescription, coding, interactions, certificates). In the United Kingdom every one of those is an empty slot waiting on a licence or a lawyer (Part 1c: the BNF for anything about medicines, a lawyer for prescriptions and certificates). What can form a British core now is the set of scores and checks that NICE, the royal colleges and the regulator ask of any doctor. That is the list below. It is for every DOCTOR role; the kit cannot yet say "every doctor role" (it can only say "every role", which includes the physiotherapist and the dietitian).

**No formula or cut-off is written here.** Each row says where it is published. Where "not fetched in this session" appears, the paper is named from the national source or from general knowledge and was not opened.

## What is missing in the kit to apply these decisions to the United Kingdom alone

The five English-speaking countries share one set of tools and one set of roles. A country can switch a tool off, rename a unit and change a tool's words. It can do nothing else. To apply this report without changing the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand at the same time, these gaps in the kit have to be closed first:

1. A country cannot change WHO SEES a tool. The roles of each tool are written once in the shared English set (countries/_dil/en/araclar/arac1-3.ts); a country can only switch a tool off (kapali), rename units (birimAdlari) or change words (degisen). The UK needs, for example, the vertigo note for general practice and neurology too.
2. A country cannot ADD a tool of its own. The list of role tools (EN_ROL_ARACLARI) and the one base tool (EN_TEMEL_ARACLAR) are shared by all five English-speaking packs. NEWS2, the acuity record or the UK concussion stages would appear in the United States pack as well.
3. A country cannot give a tool its NUMBERS. The kit leaves thresholds and intervals to the pack (parametreler), but the English set builds every tool without them (araciBicimle returns no parametreler), so none of the eight "mechanism ready" slots can be switched on for one country.
4. Country numbers are not converted to the unit the arithmetic uses. A limit for haemoglobin supplied in g/L would be compared with a value the kit has already turned into g/dL (anemi-izlem).
5. HbA1c has no unit in the kit (lab-izlem accepts 3 to 20 only): a value in mmol/mol is refused. It needs to be a laboratory quantity with a unit, like creatinine.
6. Limits in the kit that the UK states differently and cannot override: the albuminuria categories (mg/g, not mg/mmol), the hearing-loss grades and the four frequencies, the 8.5 cm range of the expected height, the BI-RADS categories of the report outline, the stage list of return to sport, the ASA class list.
7. "Base" means every role, allied professions included. There is no way to say "every DOCTOR role": a core clinical tool given as base would also show to the physiotherapist, the dietitian and the audiologist.
8. The role list is one shared set of 40 keys (lib/ulke/rolEslemesi.test.ts refuses any other key). A country can rename a role but cannot add one (geriatric medicine), drop one (the second dermatology) or split one (oncology into medical and clinical).
9. The features "my templates" and "consultations" are not switched on in any English-speaking pack, so their two base tiles do not exist here.

Until then the only decisions of this report that can be applied for the United Kingdom alone are: keeping a tool off, and changing words (the "Mild / Moderate / Severe" labels of the psoriasis score, the consent wording of the paediatric surgery list, the breath-hold wording of the inhaler list, the label of the antibiotic counter's last line).
