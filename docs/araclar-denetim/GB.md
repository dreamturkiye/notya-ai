# United Kingdom (`gb`): which doctor tools and specialties to keep, change, remove or add

Audit of 2026-10-10. Branch `araclar-denetim/gb`, from `feat/ulke-butun` at `e254ebf8`. Ordered by the owner: for this country, decide tool by tool and specialty by specialty what to keep, revise, remove or add, and propose a core set that is the United Kingdom's own. The same decisions as data: `docs/araclar-denetim/gb-kararlar.json`.

**The answer in one paragraph.** The United Kingdom build shows 42 tools today, and they are a translated Turkish set, not a British one. Seven can stay as they are, fifteen must change, and for twenty no British source could be read, so a local clinician has to look at them (they are plain checklists and date cards with nothing country-specific in the code). Eight of the live tools show a result or a label that is wrong or misleading for a British doctor; the two that matter most are the hearing-loss grade and the stages of return to sport after concussion. The four tools the pack already keeps off should stay off: one is not used here (ESI triage) and three need rebuilding on British guidance. General practice, the largest group of British doctors, sees no tool at all except the patient's page. A British core set of fifteen clinical tools is proposed, each tied to NICE, a royal college, a national body or the regulator (one pair, PHQ-9 with GAD-7, could not be tied to a source in this session), but most of the well-known scores are owned by somebody: only a few are free to build. Of the 40 roles, 8 specialty names should change to the regulator's wording, 12 recognised specialties are missing, one clinic role is a duplicate and one has no British source. The shared English kit cannot apply most of this to the United Kingdom alone today (see "What is missing in the kit").

**What this is not.** A machine read the pages named at the end. No clinician, lawyer or native editor of the United Kingdom has confirmed a line. Nothing here says the product complies with any law or standard, or that any body approves it. No code, pack, test or kit file was changed.

**How to read a verdict.** KEEP: used here as it stands. REVISE: used here, but something must change. REMOVE: not used here, superseded here, or not usable. UNVERIFIED: no source could be read in this session, so there is no verdict, only "needs a local clinician"; it is not a guess. A source in square brackets, like [NG203], is listed at the end with its address and the date it was read.

## The five findings that matter most, safety first

1. **The hearing-loss grade is the American one (switched on today).** The audiometry tool averages four frequencies and grades on a six-step American scale. The British Society of Audiology averages five frequencies and has four descriptors. A British clinician is shown "within normal limits" at an average of 22 dB (British: mild), "moderately severe" at 60 dB (no such British grade) and "profound" at 92 dB (British: severe). The asymmetry flag is not the NICE rule either. [BSA], [NG98]
2. **Return to sport does not follow the United Kingdom's concussion guideline (switched on today).** Six stages numbered 0 to 5, sport only, no timing. The UK guideline has stages 1 to 6 across school or work and sport, and fixed minimums: competition "NOT before day 21". A doctor recording "stage 5" in the tool is not recording the British stage 5. [CONCUSSION]
3. **Three mechanisms that look ready would give wrong answers if switched on with British numbers.** The kidney tool's referral flags are not NICE's (NICE refers on a kidney-failure risk above 5%, an ACR of 70 mg/mmol or more, and a fall that is both sustained and crosses a category; the code flags GFR below 30, category A3 and any 25% fall). The anaemia tool would compare limits supplied in g/L with a value it has already converted to g/dL, so every patient would read "low". The HbA1c tool refuses any value above 20, and British results are in mmol/mol (48, 53, 58). All three are off today; they must stay off until the kit changes. [NG203], [NG28]
4. **Psychiatry: no risk score and no risk level may be built.** The plan for the slots (a "safety and emergency triage", and a "safety prompt" on one questionnaire item) meets a direct instruction in NICE NG225: "Do not use risk assessment tools and scales to predict future suicide or repetition of self-harm", and no low, medium or high stratification. What NICE asks for is a psychosocial assessment and a safety plan. Build that, as a note without a score. [NG225]
5. **The build has no British core, and general practice has nothing.** The only tool every role shares is the patient's page. Eight doctor roles (General practice, General internal medicine, Gastroenterology, Obstetrics and gynaecology, Cardiology, Neurology, Psychiatry, Rehabilitation medicine) and all ten clinic roles have no tool of their own. Twenty of the 42 live tools could not be checked against any British source. The core set of Part 2 is the answer, and it depends on licences (see "Every licence problem").

Also wrong or misleading, and switched on today: the child's expected height shows a range of 8.5 cm either side where the British growth charts print 7 cm, and uses the simple rule where the college's own charts use a more accurate one [RCPCHSTAFF]; the antibiotic course counter's "last day" is one day late; the ASA picker offers "E" as if it were a class and has no class 6 [ASANHS]; the psoriasis score carries mild, moderate and severe labels that NICE does not use [CG153]; a SCORAD of exactly 50 is labelled severe [SCORAD].

## Part 1: every existing tool

Four groups: what is switched on, what this country keeps off, the empty slots every English-speaking pack shares, and two screens the kit has that this pack does not switch on. "Base" means every role sees it; "specialty" means only the named roles do.

### Tools whose result would be wrong or misleading in the United Kingdom

Switched on today:

- `asa-preop` (ASA class and pre-operative checklist): An emergency case cannot be recorded with its ASA class; class 6 cannot be recorded.
- `pasi` (PASI score): The severity label contradicts how NICE uses PASI.
- `scorad` (SCORAD index): A SCORAD of exactly 50.0 is labelled severe; the published table puts it in moderate.
- `antibiyotik-sure` (Antibiotic course: counting days): The "last day of the course" is one day later than a course counted from day 1.
- `odyometri-pta` (Pure-tone audiometry: average threshold): Grade of hearing loss and asymmetry flag follow an American scale, not the British Society of Audiology descriptors or the NICE rule.
- `vas-fonksiyon` (Pain and function rating): A severity label from an unvalidated composite.
- `hedef-boy` (Expected height from the parents' heights): The range shown is wider than the one printed on the UK growth charts, and the centre can differ from the RCPCH method.
- `rtp-basamak` (Stages of return to sport): Stage numbers and names differ from the UK guideline, and the tool is silent on the minimum 21 days.

Off today, and wrong if switched on as built:

- `kdigo-evre` (Chronic kidney disease: KDIGO categories): Referral flags and albuminuria limits are not the ones UK guidance uses.
- `kdigo-serit` (KDIGO grid: GFR and albuminuria): Albuminuria limits in mg/g, not the mg/mmol categories NICE prints.
- `rapor-taslagi` (Structured report outline): American reporting categories where UK radiologists use the 1 to 5 score with M and U prefixes.
- `lab-izlem` (HbA1c and TSH follow-up (slot, mechanism ready)): A British HbA1c value in mmol/mol is outside the range the field accepts.
- `anemi-izlem` (Anaemia follow-up in chronic kidney disease (slot, mechanism ready)): Limits supplied in g/L would be compared with a value in g/dL.
- `kardiyo-izlem` (High blood pressure, heart-failure and atrial-fibrillation follow-up (slot, mechanism ready)): One pair of limits cannot express the UK thresholds.
- `psychiatry-safety-triage` (Safety and emergency triage (slot)): If it showed a risk level, it would be what the national guideline tells clinicians not to use.

## Part 2: tools to add, and the United Kingdom's core set

**What "core" means here.** In Türkiye the tools every doctor shares are workflow tools (prescription, coding, interactions, certificates). In the United Kingdom every one of those is an empty slot waiting on a licence or a lawyer (Part 1c: the BNF for anything about medicines, a lawyer for prescriptions and certificates). What can form a British core now is the set of scores and checks that NICE, the royal colleges and the regulator ask of any doctor. That is the list below. It is for every DOCTOR role; the kit cannot yet say "every doctor role" (it can only say "every role", which includes the physiotherapist and the dietitian).

## What is missing in the kit to apply these decisions to the United Kingdom alone

The five English-speaking countries share one set of tools and one set of roles. A country can switch a tool off, rename a unit and change a tool's words. It can do nothing else. To apply this report without changing the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand at the same time, these gaps in the kit have to be closed first:

1. A country cannot change WHO SEES a tool. The roles of each tool are written once in the shared English set (countries/_dil/en/araclar/arac1-3.ts); a country can only switch a tool off (kapali), rename units (birimAdlari) or change words (degisen). The UK needs, for example, the vertigo note for general practice and neurology too.
2. A country cannot ADD a tool of its own. The list of role tools (EN_ROL_ARACLARI) and the one base tool (EN_TEMEL_ARACLAR) are shared by all five English-speaking packs. NEWS2, the acuity record or the UK concussion stages would appear in the United States pack as well.
3. A country cannot give a tool its NUMBERS. The kit leaves thresholds and intervals to the pack (parametreler), but the English set builds every tool without them (araciBicimle returns no parametreler), so none of the eight "mechanism ready" slots can be switched on for one country.
4. Country numbers are not converted to the unit the arithmetic uses. A limit for haemoglobin supplied in g/L would be compared with a value the kit has already turned into g/dL (anemi-izlem).
5. HbA1c has no unit in the kit (lab-izlem accepts 3 to 20 only): a value in mmol/mol is refused. It needs to be a laboratory quantity with a unit, like creatinine.
6. Limits in the kit that the UK states differently and cannot override: the albuminuria categories (mg/g, not mg/mmol), the hearing-loss grades and the four frequencies, the 8.5 cm range of the expected height, the BI-RADS categories of the report outline, the stage list of return to sport, the ASA class list.
7. "Base" means every role, allied professions included. There is no way to say "every DOCTOR role": a core clinical tool given as base would also show to the physiotherapist, the dietitian and the audiologist.
8. The role list is one shared set of 40 keys (lib/ulke/rolEslemesi.test.ts refuses any other key). A country can rename a role but cannot add one (geriatric medicine), drop one (the second dermatology) or split one (oncology into medical and clinical).
9. The features "my templates" and "consultations" are not switched on in any English-speaking pack, so their two base tiles do not exist here.

Until then the only decisions of this report that can be applied for the United Kingdom alone are: keeping a tool off, and changing words (the "Mild / Moderate / Severe" labels of the psoriasis score, the consent wording of the paediatric surgery list, the breath-hold wording of the inhaler list, the label of the antibiotic counter's last line).
