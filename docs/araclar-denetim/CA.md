# Doctor tools and specialties for Canada (`ca`): keep, revise, remove, add

## Second pass (2026-10-10)

The first pass ran out of its search allowance part-way: 29 of the 43 tools that are switched on were left "unverified", and several national bodies were never reached. This pass had an allowance of its own (120 searches; 104 were used) and went back to the sources. It changed this document and the data file and nothing else: no code, pack, test or kit file. Rows it touched carry `pass: 2` in `ca-kararlar.json`; in the tables of Part 1 a verdict cell that says "second pass" was changed here. Everything under the heading "First pass" is the first pass as it was written, except those verdict cells.

**How the sources were read.** Through a reading tool that returns the text of a page. Every figure taken from a source is as that tool returned it; nothing was written from memory. Before any number in the code is changed, a Canadian clinician opens the source again. Nobody in Canada (no clinician, no lawyer) has read this, and no body named here has reviewed or endorsed anything. A source in square brackets with "P2" in front, like [P2-CHMS], is in the table "Sources read in the second pass" at the end of this section; S-numbers are the first pass's.

**What was not checked again.** The arithmetic of the shared kit was checked against primary publications by the second passes of the United States, Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom, and those results are carried here by reference, not repeated: the PASI, EASI, SCORAD and DAS28 formulas; the 28 joints; the EASI weights for a child under 8 and the EASI bands; the antibiotic "last day"; the ASA class list; the kidney tool's "low risk" with no urine result; the training-ratio warning; the dose tool's trailing zeros; and which lists hold no number. They are marked "US second pass", "AU second pass" and so on: `docs/araclar-denetim/US.md` on `araclar-denetim/us` (commit `5a19decf`), `AU.md` on `araclar-denetim/au` (`db93ed45`), `NZ.md` on `araclar-denetim/nz` (`b77460ce`), `GB.md` on `araclar-denetim/gb` (`25532d81`), each under "Second pass (2026-10-10)" [P2-US], [P2-AU], [P2-NZ], [P2-GB]. The searches of this pass went to what is specific to Canada. Quebec and French stayed out of scope.

### Checked against a source and found right

- **DAS28.** Both formulas and the four bands (US second pass). In Canada the unit is the tool's: an Alberta public laboratory reports C-reactive protein in mg/L [P2-APL-CRP]. The first pass asked for a score of exactly 3.2 to move to "low"; two Canadian documents print that boundary differently (the Canadian Rheumatology Association's 2012 table, as the reading tool returned it with its layout partly garbled: low is 2.6 to 3.2; the national drug agency in 2020: low is "lower than 3.2", high is 5.1 or greater), and the code agrees with one of them at each end [P2-CRA2012], [P2-CADTH-RA]. The verdict moves from revise to keep; a rheumatologist decides what the screen says at exactly 3.20 and 5.10.
- **The 28 joints**, the **PASI formula**, **EASI for a patient of 8 or over**, the **SCORAD formula** (all US second pass).
- **The four frequencies of the hearing average.** The first pass could not find which frequencies are averaged in Canada. Statistics Canada's Canadian Health Measures Survey averages 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 kHz, the tool's four [P2-CHMS]. From 26 dB upward the tool's grades match the Canadian sheet.
- **The Canadian concussion steps**, as the first pass read them: confirmed step for step on Parachute's own table [P2-PARA-PROT].
- **Inhaler technique.** The list's one figure, a breath held for 5 to 10 seconds, is what a Health PEI sheet says; HealthLink BC says 10 seconds [P2-HPEI-MDI], [P2-HLBC-MDI].
- **Visual acuity.** The conversion is a definition; a British Columbia government page writes acuity as a fraction of 20 feet with the metric distance beside it, and the tool accepts both forms [P2-HLBC-BLIND].
- **PSA rate of change with no threshold** fits the urologists' position that the rate is not used alone in routine screening [P2-CUA-PSA].
- **Expected height: the 13 cm correction** (US second pass). Not the range: see "Still unverified".
- **The pack's laboratory units**, which the pack itself marks unverified in part: haemoglobin in g/L (British Columbia) [P2-BC-FE]; PSA in "ug/L" and C-reactive protein in mg/L (Alberta) [P2-APL-PSA], [P2-APL-CRP]; the urine albumin ratio in mg/mmol (Ontario) [P2-KW-FORM]; lipids in mmol/L on the cardiovascular society's worksheet [P2-CCS-FRS]. Each is one province or one body: not a statement about all of Canada.
- **Weight in kilograms** for the dose tool (first pass), and **removing the American triage scale** (CTAS is Canada's; its terms are below).

**What this is.** Kaan asked for every doctor tool and every specialty of the Canada build to be checked against what doctors in Canada actually use, and for a core tool set that is Canada's and not Türkiye's. A machine read the code of every tool and the Canadian sources it could open on 2026-10-10, and compared the two. **Nobody in Canada has read this report.** It changes no code, no pack, no test and no kit file. It makes no claim that the product meets any law, standard or guideline.

## The answer in one paragraph

Canada's build can show 100 tools: 43 are switched on, 55 are empty placeholders ("slots") and 2 exist in the shared kit but are not switched on here. Of the 43 that are on, **3 can stay as they are, 11 must change, and 29 could not be checked against any Canadian source** (22 of those 29 are plain record lists with no number in them). **Four of the 11 matter for safety**: the return-to-sport stages are numbered and named differently from Canada's concussion guideline, the hearing tool calls 16 to 25 dB "normal" where the Canadian sheet calls it a hearing loss, and two pieces of arithmetic in the shared kit need a clinician's ruling (a dose volume rounded to 0.1 mL, and an antibiotic course that ends a day late). One tool that is switched on prints BI-RADS categories without the licence its owner requires. One slot is not Canada's at all and should go: the ESI triage record (Canada uses CTAS). The larger problem is what is missing: **18 of the 40 roles, family medicine, internal medicine, cardiology, psychiatry and obstetrics among them, see nothing but the patient-page tile.** This report proposes a Canadian core of 14 tools for every doctor and 12 specialty tools, each tied to a Canadian body (Hypertension Canada, the Canadian Cardiovascular Society, Diabetes Canada, the Canadian Paediatric Society and others). For specialties, 26 of the 30 names match the Royal College's list, 4 need renaming, none needs removing and 6 recognised ones are proposed; of the 10 clinic roles 7 stay, 3 need renaming and 2 professions are proposed. Several of the changes cannot be made for Canada alone until the shared kit gains a few mechanisms, listed near the end.

### 1.2 Tools whose result would be wrong or misleading in Canada

Safety first. Items marked "kit" are the same in every country that uses the shared kit, Canada included.

1. **Stages of return to sport (`rtp-basamak`), on.** Stages 0 to 5 with the product's own names. Canada's guideline has steps 1 to 6 with a step 2A and 2B and requires medical clearance before step 4. The same number means a different step. S44
2. **Pure-tone audiometry (`odyometri-pta`), on.** An average of 16 to 25 dB is shown as "Within normal limits"; the Canadian Academy of Audiology's sheet grades it as a hearing loss of its own. S55
3. **Dose arithmetic by body weight (`doz-hesabi`), on, kit.** The volume for one dose is rounded to the nearest 0.1 mL and only the rounded figure is shown. For small volumes the rounding is a large share of the dose; the warning appears only below 0.1 mL. A pharmacist or pediatrician must rule on it.
4. **Antibiotic course (`antibiyotik-sure`), on, kit.** "Last day of the course" is the start date plus the number of days, which is one day after the last day if the start date is day one.
5. **PASI (`pasi`), on.** The mild / moderate / severe grade uses cut-offs no Canadian source supports; the one Canadian paper read cites a rule under which the tool's "moderate" is already severe. S58
6. **Pain and function rating (`vas-fonksiyon`), on, kit.** The grade comes from a formula the product invented; the code says so.
7. **DAS28 (`das28`), on, kit.** A score of exactly 3.2 is called moderate; the source read puts 3.2 in low activity. S56
8. **EASI (`easi`), on, kit.** The weights are for a patient aged 8 or over, and nothing stops a doctor scoring a younger child.
9. **PSA rate of change (`psa-hizi`), on.** Shown in µg/L only, while the Canadian Urological Association writes ng/mL; and no Canadian source read recommends this measure. S45
10. **The two pre-operative checklists (`genel-preop`, `cocuk-prepost-op`), on.** They count routine laboratory tests and imaging as items to complete; Choosing Wisely Canada says not to order them for low-risk surgery in patients without symptoms. S39

Already off, and would be wrong if switched on as they are: the two KDIGO tools (the kit sorts albuminuria in mg/g, Canada works in mg/mmol with its own limits, and the referral flags differ by province) and the ESI triage record (not Canada's scale).

### 2.3 Canada is unusually cautious about screening questionnaires

Five national recommendations were read that all point the same way, and they should shape how any questionnaire tool is worded here. The Canadian Task Force on Preventive Health Care recommends against screening all adults for depression with a questionnaire and a cut-off score (2025) S23, against the same in pregnancy and after birth (2022) S24, against screening children aged 1 to 4 for developmental delay with standardized tools (2016) S25, against screening older adults without symptoms for cognitive impairment (2024) S26, and against PSA screening (2014) S22. A tool that reminds a doctor to run a questionnaire on everybody would work against national guidance. The same instruments remain useful for a patient the doctor is already concerned about, and that is how they should be offered.

### 3.5 Differences between provinces, noted and not resolved

- **Vaccination schedules** are set per province and territory. S37
- **Kidney referral**: British Columbia and Ontario list different criteria; Ontario uses a kidney-failure risk score and British Columbia does not yet. S7 S8
- **Which eGFR equation the laboratory reports**: Alberta changed on 2026-01-20; Ontario had acted earlier; the other provinces were not checked. S9 S10
- **Who may call themselves a specialist, and the word "surgeon" in cosmetic work**: set by each provincial college. Ontario and British Columbia were read. S65 S66
- **Allied professions**: regulated in all ten provinces, but not in every territory. S63
- **Not read this time, known to be provincial from the earlier audit**: consent of minors, laboratory units and report formats, billing codes, notifiable diseases, prescription forms.
- **Quebec and French**: out of scope. This build is English only; Quebec certifies its own specialists and needs French. Nothing in this report was checked for Quebec.
