## Option A — safe

**Headline:** More than a scribe. A clinical assistant that knows the file.
**Subhead:** Notya records the visit with your patient's consent and drafts the note for you to approve — then helps you find what is already in the patient's record, keeps paediatric measurements in order and gives each patient a page of their own.

Proof points:
1. **Ask about the file, out loud.** Ask the voice assistant what is already in the patient's file — last visit, recorded medicines, past measurements — and hear it read back, with the full text on your screen. It retrieves; you decide.
2. **Paediatric depth, built in.** Growth measurements plotted on charts, the Australian immunisation schedule laid out on the child's file and a target-height record, alongside the note. Each tool shows its source.
3. **A page for every patient.** Patients fill in their form before the visit, ask for appointments and record their privacy consent on their own page. Nothing is sent without your say.

## Option B — bolder

**Headline:** Your notes are the start. Notya knows the whole file.
**Subhead:** Scribes stop at the note. Notya drafts it for your approval, then answers from the patient's own record when you ask, follows a child's growth and vaccines visit to visit, and keeps the patient connected between appointments.

Proof points:
1. **"What did we record last time?" — answered in seconds.** The voice assistant reads back what is in the patient's file, out loud and on screen. No searching through old notes.
2. **Made for children's doctors.** Growth charts, the Australian immunisation schedule and target height on every child's file, kept up to date as you see them.
3. **Patients stay with you between visits.** Their own page for forms, appointment requests and privacy requests, with a record you can show.

Notes for Kaan: B's "in seconds" is a speed claim — keep it only if we can measure it on the AU build. "Scribes stop at the note" names no competitor but is comparative; the lawyer should confirm it is fair. Both options describe the voice assistant as retrieving from the file, not giving clinical answers (memo section 3).
