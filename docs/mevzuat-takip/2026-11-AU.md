## Items

### 1. Privacy policies must explain automated decision-making from 10 December 2026 (APP 1.7–1.9)

- **What changed:** The OAIC published an APP 1.7–1.9 Transparency Obligation Fact Sheet, an agency supplement and a flowchart, and updated the APP 1 Guidelines. From 10 December 2026, an APP entity must state three things in its privacy policy: the kinds of personal information used in such programs, the kinds of decisions made solely by them, and the kinds of decisions where the program does something "substantially and directly related" to making the decision. The rule applies when the decision "could reasonably be expected to significantly affect the rights or interests of an individual". The fact sheet lists "computer programs used to prioritise the provision of health or disability services to individuals" as generally in scope. One worked example is a health provider that ranks patients by blood pressure and BMI. The fact sheet also says a decision "may be within scope … even where a computer program output does not replace the entire decision-making process or is subject to human review", and that generative AI output is generally in scope "unless subject to extensive human oversight and control".
- **Official reference:** OAIC, "New resources on transparency for use of AI and automated decision-making", 30 Sep 2026, https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/new-resources-on-transparency-for-use-of-ai-and-automated-decision-making (opened). OAIC, APP 1.7–1.9 Transparency Obligation Fact Sheet, September 2026, https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/269013/APP-1.7-1.9-Transparency-Obligation-Fact-Sheet.PDF (opened). The obligation comes from the Privacy Act 1988 as amended in 2024. I did not open the amending Act this run.
- **In force / planned:** 10 December 2026.
- **Touches in Notya (au):** The AU privacy policy draft (`countries/au/yasal.ts` → shared `lib/ulke/yasal/ingilizce`) has no ADM section. Features that may be relevant:
  - the AI note and assistant (Anthropic Claude);
  - the follow-up list, examination queue and critical-conditions checklist, if they rank or flag patients.
- **Proposed action:** Ask the AU lawyer, before 10 December, whether Notya (as an APP entity for its portal and reminders) or the clinics using it fall under APP 1.7. If either does, add an ADM paragraph to the AU privacy draft that lists the personal information used and the kinds of decisions assisted. That would be an AU-only legal text; the shared template's layout stays the same. Keep the current "review this draft" wording until the lawyer has looked at it.
- **Product change needed:** yes (AU privacy draft text only, pending the lawyer's scope assessment). Logged in `docs/OPEN-COMMITMENTS.md`, waiting on Kaan.
- **Urgency:** now (commences 10 Dec 2026).

### 2. TGA digital scribe review: outcome published, page could not be read

- **What changed:** The TGA has a page titled "Outcome of digital scribe review" (also listed as "Digital scribe review outcomes") on tga.gov.au. I saw only the title and could not confirm its date. The TGA "Digital Scribes – Stakeholder Form" (opened 25 Nov 2025, closes 31 Dec 2026) says the features that "may meet the definition of a medical device" are clinical inferences, recommendations and diagnostic suggestions. A product found "likely a medical device" may need its supply stopped, or those features removed, until it is included on the ARTG. A TGA presentation from July 2026, which falls before the period, says a scribe that only transcribes, edits or summarises for clinician review is not a medical device. It says a scribe that generates diagnoses, differential diagnoses, treatment recommendations or prognoses may be one. The same presentation lists as "upcoming" a systematic review of clinical decision support software (CDSS) that has been notified as exempt.
- **Official reference:**
  - TGA, "Outcome of digital scribe review", https://www.tga.gov.au/node/599817 and https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/publication/corporate-reports/digital-scribe-review-outcomes (title only, not opened: robots_disallowed).
  - TGA Consultation Hub, "Digital Scribes – Stakeholder Form", https://consultations.tga.gov.au/tga/digital-scribes-stakeholder-form/ (opened).
  - TGA, "Software and AI compliance and reforms" (G. Harradine), July 2026, https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/software-and-ai-compliance-and-reforms.pdf (opened; before the period).
- **In force / planned:** This is the existing definition of a medical device applied to scribes. No new instrument was found. Compliance action follows case by case.

### 5. Privacy Act reform Bill: exposure draft consultation closed 18 September 2026

- **What changed:** The Attorney-General's Department consulted on the exposure draft of the *Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026* from 31 Aug to 18 Sep 2026. The consultation paper proposes:
  - a single "fair and reasonable" test in place of APPs 3, 4 and 6;
  - consent to collect sensitive information;
  - reporting eligible breaches to the Commissioner within 72 hours;
  - a controller/processor split;
  - treating information generated or derived by AI as "collected";
  - adding genomic information to health information.

  It gives no commencement date. The Bill is still subject to government consideration.
- **Official reference:** AGD Consultation Hub, "Privacy Reform", https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform/ (opened). Consultation paper: https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform/user_uploads/consultation_paper.pdf (opened, first ~100k characters).
- **In force / planned:** Not introduced to Parliament. No date.
- **Touches in Notya (au):** Nothing today. Later it would affect the AU privacy draft, breach handling (72 hours) and the processor role for clinics.
- **Proposed action:** Watch for the Bill's introduction, and tell the AU lawyer about it alongside item 1.
