## Items

### 1. OPC Children's Privacy Code (guidance under PIPEDA)

- **What changed:** The Office of the Privacy Commissioner (OPC) published Canada's first Children's Privacy Code. It covers organisations subject to PIPEDA that run online services directed at children *or likely to be accessed by them*, even when children are not the intended audience. It treats anyone under 18 as a child and scales duties by age band (0–5 to 16–17). It calls for meaningful consent suited to capacity, parental consent under 13 in all but exceptional cases, and opt-in for non-essential uses; consent cannot be inferred from inaction. Other expectations: a way for the child to correct and fully delete their data, no profiling for marketing or AI-driven decisions, privacy-protective defaults (location, profile visibility and third-party sharing off), no deceptive design, data minimisation, set retention periods, and privacy impact assessments "strongly encouraged". Health and biometric data are named as sensitive information that needs express consent. There is no section specific to health services or patient portals. The Code does not call itself binding; it presents the OPC's view of how PIPEDA applies to children.
- **Official reference:** OPC news release, 2026-10-20, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/nr-c_261020/ (opened). Code text: "Children's Privacy Code", issued 2026-10-20, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/protection-of-children-for-businesses/childrens-privacy-code/children-code-final-report-2026/ (opened). Comments open until 2027-01-20.
- **In force / planned:** Published 2026-10-20 as guidance; no effective date stated. It may be updated after the comment period.
- **Touches in Notya (`ca`):** the CA privacy-policy draft (`countries/ca/yasal.ts` on `feat/ulke-butun`), which names PIPEDA and provincial statutes but says nothing about minors. Also the patient portal and appointment reminders where the patient is under 18, which is the same population the shared `veliDiliMi` (parent wording) logic covers.
- **Proposed action:** Add a line to the lawyer-review note on the CA privacy draft: "review against OPC Children's Privacy Code (2026-10-20), incl. parental consent under 13, age-appropriate notices, deletion, no AI profiling of minors." Have counsel decide whether a clinic-facing clinical tool whose portal minors may reach is "likely to be accessed by children". Consider whether Notya should file a comment by 2027-01-20 (owner decision).
- **Product change needed:** yes (CA legal draft text only: the lawyer-review note in `countries/ca/yasal.ts`; no logic change until counsel advises). Logged in `docs/OPEN-COMMITMENTS.md`, waiting on Kaan.

### 2. Bill C-36: Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act (federal private-sector privacy reform)

- **What changed in the period:** Nothing. The bill was introduced at first reading on 2026-06-15. LEGISinfo shows it "at second reading in the House of Commons" with no second-reading activity. A Charter Statement was tabled on 2026-08-19. According to the government backgrounder, the bill would require transparency about automated decision systems, including AI ones. Organisations would have to assess and mitigate privacy risks before sending personal information outside Canada, and would face a higher standard for children's information. It sets rules on de-identification and anonymisation and creates a Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission. Administrative penalties would reach $10M or 3% of global revenue, and fines $25M or 5%. It is a privacy law, not a separate AI act.
- **Official reference:** Bill C-36 (45-1), first reading 2026-06-15, LEGISinfo https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-36 (opened). ISED backgrounder, 2026-06-15, https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/06/government-of-canada-introduces-legislation-to-protect-canadians-privacy-in-the-digital-age.html (opened).
- **In force / planned:** Not enacted; no date.
- **Touches in Notya (`ca`):** if passed, the CA privacy draft would need to cover cross-border transfer (Notya sends note text to Anthropic, outside Canada) and automated-decision transparency (AI notes and assistant). Nothing today.
- **Proposed action:** Track at each run. Start redrafting only when the bill passes second reading or committee.

### 5. OPC updated guidance on recording calls (voiceprints as sensitive biometric data)

- **What changed:** The OPC updated its guidance for organisations that record customer calls. PIPEDA applies whichever party starts the call. The update clarifies when meaningful consent may be required and treats voiceprints as sensitive biometric information. Organisations that use third-party call centres must make sure those providers follow the rules.
- **Official reference:** OPC news release, 2026-10-08, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/nr-c_261008/ (opened). Guidance page https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/surveillance/02_05_d_14/ (not opened).
- **In force / planned:** Published 2026-10-08 (guidance).
- **Touches in Notya (`ca`):** It is aimed at customer-service calls, not clinical dictation. Its biometric (voiceprint) and third-party-processor points are relevant by analogy to the CA privacy draft if Notya stores consultation audio or sends it to a processor. Nothing in the product changes. (Any change to the voice path is under the founder lock in any case.)
- **Proposed action:** Have counsel read the guidance page when reviewing the CA privacy draft's section on audio and processors.
- **Product change needed:** no
