## Items

### 1. ICO replaced by the Information Commission; complaints duty on controllers

- **What changed:** On 30 September 2026 the Information Commission, a body corporate, took over the ICO's functions. The government says the "transition does not change the regulator's role, responsibilities, or powers." Before the period, on 23 June 2026, a new duty started: organisations must give people "a clear way to raise a data protection complaint", "acknowledge it within 30 days", look into the complaint and tell the person the outcome.
- **Official reference:** GOV.UK news "Information Commission succeeds the ICO as UK's data protection regulator", 30 September 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/information-commission-succeeds-the-ico-as-uks-data-protection-regulator (opened; it gives no SI number). DSIT "Data Use and Access Act 2025: plans for commencement", updated 12 October 2026, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-plans-for-commencement (opened). That page lists the transfer on 30 September 2026 under Commencement No. 9 and no further commencement after 2 November 2026. ICO "New data protection complaints law now in force", June 2026, https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/ (opened). The Act is the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- **In force / planned:** Regulator change in force 30 September 2026. Complaints duty in force 23 June 2026.
- **Touches in Notya (gb):** The GB legal draft (`countries/gb/yasal.ts` with the shared `lib/ulke/yasal/ingilizce.ts` on `feat/ulke-butun`). The privacy text gives an email address for access, correction and deletion requests. It names no regulator and describes no complaint route or acknowledgement time.
- **Proposed action:** Add to the GB privacy draft, or to its lawyer-review note, a data-protection complaint route with a 30-day acknowledgement. Name the regulator as the "Information Commission", not the ICO, wherever the lawyer adds the right to complain. Keep the shared English kit free of statute names, as it is now. Put the GB wording in the GB pack.
- **Product change needed:** yes (GB legal draft text only). Logged in `docs/OPEN-COMMITMENTS.md`, waiting on Kaan.
- **Urgency:** this month

### 2. Government response on regulating AI in healthcare (all 44 recommendations accepted)

- **What changed:** The government accepted all 44 recommendations of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare. Recommendation 1 says an updated medical-device definition will clarify when some decision-support software is not a medical device. The MHRA will consult on a new approach to how software and AI qualify and are classified. Secondary legislation would then update the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002: the device definition, the classification rules and "intended purpose". The interim guidance on stand-alone software will be updated first. Under Recommendation 31, DHSC and the MHRA will publish technology-specific guidance, using existing work on Ambient Voice Technology as the model. A working group will clarify who is responsible for what, and NHS England and NHS Resolution will continue work on indemnity. Dates given: draft PCCP guidance in December 2026; the classification and qualification consultation and an implementation roadmap in spring 2027. For context, outside the period: NHS England's letter of 29 July 2026 (PRN02607_i) points to MHRA guidance. Under it, products "solely intended to transcribe and summarise clinical conversations, draft correspondence or suggest clinical codes for review by a clinician" are not medical devices. Products "intended to support diagnosis, treatment and automated clinical actions" are.
- **Official reference:** MHRA/DHSC "Government Response to the National Commission's Recommendations on the Regulation of AI in Healthcare", published 6 October 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-response-to-the-national-commissions-recommendations-on-the-regulation-of-ai-in-healthcare/government-response-to-the-national-commissions-recommendations-on-the-regulation-of-ai-in-healthcare (opened; the last 23,000 characters, part of Annex A, were not read). NHS England "Medical device regulation for ambient voice technology products", 29 July 2026, https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/medical-device-regulation-for-ambient-voice-technology-products/ (opened). MHRA "Software and AI as a medical device" shows a visible update date of 3 February 2025, with no 2026 change on the page: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device (opened).
- **In force / planned:** Policy only, nothing binding yet. Draft PCCP guidance due December 2026. Consultation due spring 2027, with a statutory instrument after it.
- **Touches in Notya (gb):** The GB medical-device marketing statement in the legal draft. It is also relevant to the qualification of the note and assistant features. Note-taking alone sits on the "not a device" side of the July 2026 line. Score tools such as DAS28, PASI and EASI, and any assistant output that supports diagnosis or treatment, need the device question answered by a regulatory adviser. Nothing changes today.
- **Proposed action:** Record in the GB lawyer and regulatory pack that the AVT line (scribe is not a device; diagnosis or treatment support is) is the current MHRA position. Recheck in December 2026 (PCCP draft) and spring 2027 (classification consultation, worth a response).
- **Product change needed:** no
- **Urgency:** watch

### 5. GMC reform: "physician assistant" title and a single register (still pending)

- **What changed (status only):** Nothing in the period. The DHSC consultation "Reforming the General Medical Council legislative framework" ran from 24 March to 21 July 2026, and no government response has been published ("We are analysing your feedback"). It proposes renaming "physician associate" to "physician assistant", protecting titles including "Physician", "Surgeon" and "General practitioner", and replacing the specialist and GP registers with a single register that uses "enhancements". The list of recognised specialties in the PMET Order would be revoked and set instead by Order of Council.
- **Official reference:** https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/reforming-the-general-medical-council-legislative-framework (opened); consultation document https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/reforming-the-general-medical-council-legislative-framework/reforming-the-general-medical-council-legislative-framework-consultation-document (opened, first 100,000 characters).
- **In force / planned:** Not yet. Draft order after the government response. Title protection would start six months after the order comes into force.
- **Touches in Notya (gb):** The GB specialty list, which is based on the GMC curricula, and the role and title labels. Nothing changes today.
- **Proposed action:** Recheck at each run. When the order is laid, review the specialty source (PMET Order list versus Order of Council categories) and any "physician associate/assistant" wording.
- **Product change needed:** no
- **Urgency:** watch
